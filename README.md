# Upskill Assistant

**An AI-assisted learning and career preparation platform for IT professionals in Moldova.**

Upskill Assistant is being designed to help IT professionals affected by layoffs or project closures identify skill gaps, expand their technical stack, improve professional English, and prepare for their next role.

**Status:** project concept and pilot planning. The features below are planned; a public application is not yet available. This page introduces the project during preparation for the Moldova Innovation Fund competition.

## The problem

Across the global IT industry, roles and skill requirements are changing, while layoffs and project closures leave professionals facing uncertain career transitions. For those affected in Moldova, returning to work may require learning a new technology stack, improving professional English, or preparing for a different role.

After losing a job, deciding what to learn and how to demonstrate new skills can be difficult, particularly under financial pressure and with reduced confidence. Courses, job listings, language practice, and interview preparation are often spread across separate tools. Upskill Assistant aims to help displaced IT professionals navigate these challenges with an individual learning plan, practical exercises, and feedback as they prepare to return to employment.

## The proposed experience

1. **Understand the starting point.** Assess technical experience, English, career goals, and areas that need improvement.
2. **Build a personal plan.** Suggest a sequence of learning activities and practical exercises for a target role or technology stack.
3. **Practise with feedback.** Offer technical exercises, voice-based mock interviews, professional English practice, and workplace communication scenarios.
4. **Receive regular vacancy digests.** Get recurring online updates with relevant vacancies matched to skills, career goals, and learning progress, including links to the original listings. Compare job requirements with the participant’s profile to prioritise the remaining preparation. Vacancy sources and collection methods will be evaluated during prototyping; options include job-board APIs, permitted feeds, and employer-provided listings. Sources and delivery frequency are yet to be selected.
5. **Track progress and applications.** Review completed activities, assessment results, and interview feedback, and record job applications and their status in one place.

## Planned pilot

The proposed six-month project will develop and test an MVP with **10–20 IT professionals in Moldova**. The intended product includes a web application, Android and iOS clients, and a shared backend. The first release will focus on skill assessment, a personal learning plan, and interview practice.

The pilot will evaluate:

- participant engagement and completion of planned learning activities;
- changes in assessed skills and interview performance;
- usefulness and quality of AI feedback;
- operating cost per active participant, including voice sessions.

These are planned evaluation areas, not achieved results. Employment outcomes may be followed up, but the platform does not promise job placement.

## Technology and cost control

The proposed stack uses **Kotlin Multiplatform**, a Kotlin backend, and external AI APIs. Models will be selected according to task complexity: lower-cost models for routine activities and more capable models for demanding assessments and feedback. Usage limits and cost monitoring will keep the pilot within its AI budget.

Local or open-weight models may be evaluated where their quality and deployment costs make them suitable. Final implementation choices will be validated during prototyping.

## Potential public-sector adoption

If the pilot demonstrates useful outcomes and sustainable operating costs, the platform could be offered for adoption or transfer to Moldovan public authorities responsible for employment and social policy. This could support wider access to personalised reskilling and career preparation through public employment services.

One possible future integration is with employment and social services that provide training for people who have lost their jobs. The platform could deliver personalised training online, allowing participants to learn remotely while authorised advisers track their progress, identify where additional support is needed, and follow up with them.

A future adviser dashboard could show agreed learning goals, completed activities, assessment results, and job-search progress, with participant consent and appropriate access controls. The platform could automatically summarise recorded learning activity and applications logged by participants or received through supported integrations, distinguishing self-reported information from verified records.

Where an employment service accepts digital reporting, these online updates could replace some routine in-person progress reports about training and job applications. This could reduce travel and administrative effort for participants, lower routine workload at service offices, and give advisers more time to support people who need individual assistance. The extent to which visits could be reduced would be agreed with the responsible service.

Programme-level reporting could help services evaluate participation and training outcomes. These public-service capabilities are proposed for a later phase and would be shaped with a public-sector partner.

Any transfer, including its scope, ownership or licensing arrangements, and ongoing operation and support, would be subject to agreement with the interested authority. This is a proposed future pathway; no government partnership or commitment is currently claimed.
